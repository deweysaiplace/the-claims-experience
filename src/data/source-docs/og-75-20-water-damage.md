## I. PURPOSE

This Operation Guide (OG) provides guidelines for handling water damage claims under the
Homeowner Policy forms consistent with Our Commitment to Our Policyholders and the Preface. In
general, the guidance provided is applicable to all homeowner policy forms unless otherwise
indicated in the OG. Review the policy form and/or endorsement(s) to confirm the specific
contract language and/or limits which are applicable to an individual claim.

If a policy provision or application is more restrictive than the law of the jurisdiction, then
the law of the jurisdiction will apply. Also, there may be situations where the Enterprise
position is broader than the law of the jurisdiction. In such situations, you should follow the
Enterprise position. If policy wording in the jurisdiction conflicts with any of these
guidelines or in case of unusual claim situations, contact claim management.

## II. FLOOD, SURFACE WATER, AND SUBSURFACE WATER EXCLUSION

### A. HW 2100 Series

**SECTION I – LOSSES NOT INSURED**, 2. c. states what type of water or water damage is excluded.
This exclusion applies to **Coverage A – Dwelling** and **Coverage B – Personal Property**:

> *2.* ***We*** *will not pay for, under any part of this policy, any loss that would not have
> occurred in the absence of one or more of the following excluded events.* ***We*** *will not
> pay for such loss regardless of: (a) the cause of the excluded event; or (b) other causes of
> loss; or (c) whether other causes acted concurrently or in any sequence with the excluded
> event to produce the loss; or (d) whether the event occurs abruptly or gradually, involves
> isolated or widespread damage, arises from natural or external forces, or occurs as a result
> of any combination of these:*
>
> c. Water, meaning:
> (1) flood;
> (2) surface water. This does not include water solely caused by the release of water from a
> swimming pool, spigot, sprinkler system, hose, or hydrant;
> (3) waves (including tidal wave, tsunami, and seiche);
> (4) tides or tidal water
> (5) overflow of any body of water (including any release, escape, or rising of any body of
> water, or any water held, contained, controlled, or diverted by a dam, levee, dike, or any
> type of water containment, diversion, or flood control device);
> (6) spray or surge from any of the items c.(1) through c.(5) described above, all whether
> driven by wind or not;
> (7) water or sewage from outside the ***residence premises*** plumbing system that enters
> through sewers or drains, or water or sewage that enters into and overflows from with a sump
> pump, sump pump well, or any other system designed to remove subsurface water that is drained
> from the foundation area;
> (8) water or sewage below the surface of the ground, including water or sewage that exerts
> pressure on, or seeps or leaks through a ***building structure***, sidewalk, driveway,
> swimming pool, or other structure; or

Loss caused by subsurface water or sewage is not covered, regardless of the source.

> *(9) material carried or otherwise moved by any of the water or sewage, as described in items
> c.(1) through c.(8) above.*

However, **we** will pay for any accidental direct physical loss by fire, explosion, or theft
resulting from water, provided the resulting loss is itself a ***loss insured***.

There is a limited exception to this exclusion for ensuing damage. The exception grants coverage
back for a direct loss by fire, explosion, or theft resulting from water damage.

Generally, a loss caused by water that enters the dwelling from the surface of the ground which
originated from a domestic source is not considered surface water and therefore, **Section I –
Losses Not Insured**, 2.c.(2) would not apply. In case of unusual claim situations, consult with
claim management.

Subsurface water originating from any source is excluded.

## III. WATER LOSSES - TEAR OUT

### A. HW 2100 Homeowner Series

1. Tear Out - Access to Plumbing System

   The HW 2100 Homeowner Series provides coverage for tear out and replacement to the
   ***building structure***, only when a covered loss or damage occurs to Coverage A property.
   This is applicable if loss to covered property is caused by water, steam or sewage, not
   otherwise excluded. Tear out and replacement applies only to that particular part of the
   building necessary to gain access to the specific point of the plumbing system or appliance
   where the water, steam or sewage escaped.

   The cost of repairing or replacing the system or appliance from which the water, steam or
   sewage escaped is not included as part of the tear out. There is no coverage for the system
   or appliance from which the water or steam escaped unless these items are damaged by a loss
   insured.

   At times, it may be difficult to determine exactly which components make up the "system" or
   "appliance." Consult with management in such cases.

2. Tear Out - Other Structures

   We pay for tear out and replacement of driveways, sidewalks, patios, and other structures
   that qualify as dwelling extensions only if damage to the plumbing system or appliance is a
   covered loss, and tear out of the driveway, sidewalk, is necessary for repairs.

   If the policy is endorsed by the Home Systems Protection endorsement or Service Line Coverage
   endorsement, review OG 75-156 Home System Protection and Service Line Coverage Endorsements
   for guidance on coverage.

3. Investigative Tear In

   In some cases where a coverage decision cannot be made by asking questions, the claim handler
   can consider payment for investigative tear out to obtain additional information needed to
   evaluate coverage.

   For example, tear out and replacement of concrete slabs when the leak is in a slab, requires
   evaluation on a case-by-case basis. The presence of a "hot spot" in a slab with no resulting
   damage is not sufficient evidence of a covered loss to trigger coverage for tear out. First
   determine whether a covered event, such as lightning or freezing, caused the pipe to leak.
   Damage to plumbing can occur because of gradual deterioration of the water pipe or
   electrolysis which would not be covered. If the inspection reveals no damage to the plumbing
   caused by a covered loss, we will only pay the cost of the tear out for inspection purposes.
   We will not pay to repair the damage caused by the inspection. Communicate this position to
   the policyholder before any work begins.

   If the policy includes the Home Systems Protection endorsement refer to OG 75-156 Home System
   Protection and Service Line Coverage Endorsements for further guidance related to potential
   coverage for this loss.

   Consult management for guidance in questionable situations. Consider use of a non-waiver or
   reservation of rights where causation may not trigger coverage.

## IV. DISCHARGE OR OVERFLOW OF WATER

Abrupt and accidental discharge or overflow (HW 2100) is a named peril for **Coverage B -
Personal Property**.

Consider the following when evaluating coverage under this peril:

- A waterbed is not considered a household appliance.
- There is no coverage for damage to personal property caused by sewer or drain back-up, or sump
  pump overflow or failure. Water or sewage entering from outside the residence premises
  plumbing system is excluded. As with building coverage, we must distinguish between "back-ups"
  and "fill ups." Check for the presence of a Back-up of Sewer and Drain endorsement. If
  applicable, further coverage analysis is needed.

## VI. BACK-UP OF SEWER OR DRAIN

Under the Homeowners Policy there is no coverage for damage caused by sewer or drain back-up, or
sump pump overflow or failure. Water or sewage entering from outside the residence premises
plumbing system is excluded except as discussed below.

### A. Drain Blockage and Fill-Up

Coverage questions have arisen when a drain or sewer line on an insured premise becomes plugged
or blocked for various reasons. Domestic water then fills up behind this blocked drain from
within the interior plumbing system. As a result of this blockage, the drain line fills up to the
point of origin and overflows.

Example: A drain trap underneath a lavatory becomes plugged and the faucet is accidentally left
on, which causes the bowl to overflow. This is a "fill-up" situation and coverage should be
extended for this accidental discharge, leakage, or overflow of water.

For coverage to apply, domestic water from an internal source must fill up through the sewer or
drain due to blockage of the system.

When a loss does not fall within the above guidelines, make no commitment, but consult
management for guidance in questionable situations.

### B. Septic Systems

A septic system is considered a part of the dwelling's plumbing system and is a sewer and/or
drain. The coverage, exclusions, and endorsements that apply to sewers and drains also apply to
septic systems. However, since septic systems are typically wholly contained within the residence
premises plumbing system, the exclusion for "water or sewage from outside the residence premises
plumbing system that enters through sewers or drains..." would not usually apply to a septic
system.

When the insured residence premises has a self-contained septic system, apply the Homeowners
Policy as follows:

- Water or sewage from the septic system (whether combined with subsurface water or not) that
  enters the insured dwelling through the connected plumbing system is not excluded by **Losses
  Not Insured** 2.c.(7) (HW 2100). Therefore, coverage will apply.
- Where excluded surface water causes the water or sewage from the septic system to enter the
  insured dwelling through the connected plumbing system, **Losses Not Insured** 2.c.(1-6) (HW
  2100) would apply. For example, if there is a general condition of flooding covering the
  premises and this flood water inundates the septic system causing it to back up into the
  residence, coverage would not apply.
- Damage caused by a septic system that backs up because of the presence of tree roots is
  excluded by **Losses Not Insured** 1.m. (HW 2100). There is no coverage for the resulting loss.
- However, a back-up of sewer or drain endorsement may provide coverage. If the policy includes a
  back-up of sewer or drain endorsement, further coverage analysis is needed.

  Refer to section D. Tree Roots.

### C. Sump Pump Inoperative

If a sump pump fails, and subsurface water enters the building (or building structure) and causes
damage, such water damage is excluded by **Losses Not Insured** exclusions regardless of the peril
causing damage to the sump pump. However, damage to the sump pump itself, if caused by a peril
not otherwise excluded, may be a covered loss.

If the policy is endorsed by the Home Systems Protection endorsement or Service Line Coverage
endorsement, review OG 75-156 Home Systems Protection and Service Line Coverage Endorsements, for
guidance.

### D. Tree Roots

The Homeowners policy does not provide coverage for damage to **Coverage A – Dwelling** property
caused by pressure from or the presence of tree, shrub, or plant roots. There is no coverage for
any resulting **Coverage A – Dwelling** loss due to pressure from or the presence of tree, shrub,
or plant roots.

1. HW 2100 Homeowners Series

   Check for the presence of a BUSD endorsement. If applicable, **Losses Not Insured** 1.m.
   which applies to **Coverage A – Dwelling** only, does not affect or apply to water damage
   otherwise covered under the BUSD endorsement.

   If the insured has the BUSD endorsement, although the tree roots could be considered the
   proximate cause of the water damage from the BUSD, the Company position is to pay for this
   water damage subject to the BUSD endorsement provisions.

   - This coverage position does not include the expense for removal of tree roots as a part of
     the loss.
   - The damage to the drain tile caused by the tree roots and removal of the tree roots from the
     drain tile are excluded.

   If water damage occurs due to the presence or pressure of roots and there is no BUSD
   endorsement on the policy, **Losses Not Insured** 1.m. applies, and there is no coverage for
   the water damage.

   There may be a specific law in your jurisdiction that would require a different causation
   analysis, and you should follow the law of your state.

   If the policy is endorsed by the Home Systems Protection endorsement or Service Line Coverage
   endorsement, review OG 75-156 Home System Protection and Service Line Coverage Endorsements
   for guidance.

   Personal Property

   If personal property items are involved in the loss, refer to **Coverage B – Personal
   Property** named perils for further coverage analysis.

   If the insured has the BUSD endorsement, although the tree roots could be considered the
   proximate cause of the water damage from the backup of sewer or drain, the Company position is
   to pay for this water damage, subject to the BUSD endorsement provisions.

### E. Additional Living Expenses

When a covered BUSD causes the residence premises to become uninhabitable, Loss of Use coverage
including ALE is applicable and all policy provisions. In many endorsements, ALE amounts payable
are included in and limited by the BUSD endorsement special limit. The amount payable for ALE is
described in the policy language and is limited to the period of time required to repair the
covered sewer or drain loss damage, but not to exceed the other time or dollar limitations in the
BUSD endorsement **Coverage C – Loss of Use, Additional Living Expense**.

If there is evidence a covered Back-up of Sewer or Drain (BUSD) loss occurred, which caused the
residence premises to be uninhabitable, followed by a general condition of flooding, ALE may be
extended subject to policy limitations. The reasonable time necessary to repair the BUSD loss
damage may be extended while the customer is waiting on NFIP to provide paperwork to State Farm
to reconcile the BUSD claim.

If a flood claim covered by a NFIP policy occurs and there is evidence that a covered Back-up of
Sewer or Drain (BUSD) loss occurred as well, (which caused the residence premises to be
uninhabitable), ALE may be extended subject to policy limitations while the customer is waiting
on NFIP to provide paperwork to State Farm so the BUSD claim can be adjusted. ALE would be
available only for the reasonable time necessary to repair the BUSD loss damage, including the
time it takes to secure and reconcile the NFIP documents.

Refer to OG 75-102 Loss of Use Coverage Additional Living Expenses, Fair Rental Value and
Prohibited Use: Homeowners Policy.

### F. Back-up of Sewer or Drain Endorsement

Back-up of sewer or drain endorsements are available in some states. Applicable endorsements for
each state should be reviewed to verify coverage, special limits and deductibles.

A typical back-up of sewer or drain endorsement provides coverage for accidental direct physical
damage to covered property caused by or resulting from water or sewage from outside the residence
premises plumbing system that enters through interior sewers or drains, or water that enters into
and overflows from within a sump pump, sump pump well, or any other system designed to remove
subsurface water which is drained from the foundation area.

1. HW 2100 Homeowner Series

   When a policy includes a back-up of sewer or drain endorsement, a separate back-up of sewer or
   drain loss is covered even if the back-up is caused by flooding, or the other types of water
   damage specified in paragraph 2.c.(1).through (5), provided the water enters the dwelling
   through the sewer or drain. There is not coverage under the endorsement for water entering the
   dwelling through any other point. For example, no coverage would be available for natural
   surface water entering the dwelling through a window well, or subsurface water entering a
   dwelling through the foundation floor or basement wall. Coverage is provided subject to the
   limits of the endorsement/policy (whichever is applicable).

   When the back-up of sewer or drain and flood, surface water and/or water below the surface of
   the ground act together to cause the damage, the loss would be excluded under **Section I –
   Losses Not Insured**, 2. c. **Water Damage**, paragraph (1).through (5), and/or (8), (9).

   When there is a covered sewer or drain loss and an excluded water damage loss, it is the
   responsibility of the claim handler to adjust the covered sewer or drain loss with the
   policyholder. It is recommended that management be consulted on any disputed losses.

   Investigation of the extent of covered sewer or drain loss will determine the damages owed
   under the sewer or drain endorsement.

   When there is no evidence of damage from a covered sewer or drain loss prior to the damage
   caused by flood/surface water/water below the surface of the ground, the damage may be
   excluded by **Section I – Losses Not Insured** 2. c. **Water**

2. Other Insurance - National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)

   Some sewer or drain endorsements contain an "other insurance" clause that indicates the
   coverage is excess over other valid and collectible insurance, which would include flood
   insurance. Review policy and state endorsements.

   The National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) - Standard Flood Insurance Policy (SFIP) may
   provide coverage for Backup of Sewer or Drain (BUSD) losses when a flood as defined by the
   Standard Flood Insurance Policy is the proximate cause of the loss.

   This may result in duplicate coverage for policyholders who have a NFIP policy and a State
   Farm policy endorsement which covers the BUSD loss.

   When the BUSD loss is covered by both a NFIP policy and a State Farm Backup of Sewer or Drain
   endorsement, the NFIP policy is primary. The State Farm BUSD endorsement is excess insurance
   as stated in the Other Insurance provision of the BUSD endorsements.

   For BUSD losses covered by both a NFIP policy and the State Farm policy, claim handlers should
   investigate the loss and advise the policyholder of the need to obtain the NFIP settlement
   documents before the claim can be finalized.

   Additional Living Expenses may be applicable if the BUSD causes the home to be uninhabitable.

   Refer to Section VI. Back-up of Sewer or Drain E. Additional Living Expenses above.

   Once NFIP settlement documentation is received, claim handlers should evaluate the loss and
   determine what additional amounts, if any, (including consideration of the NFIP deductible)
   can be paid under the policy.

   Claim handlers should not ask State Farm agents for any information regarding NFIP direct
   losses.

   Additionally, some sewer or drain endorsements contain a waiting period. If there are any
   questions on when the waiting period begins or ends, contact Personal Lines Fire at
   (844) 275-7522.

## VII. CLAIMS FROM FREEZING

### A. Coverage A – Dwelling Frozen Pipes

1. HW 2100 Homeowners Series

   When evaluating coverage for a claim resulting from frozen pipes review **Section – I Losses
   not Insured** 1. b. which excludes **Coverage A – Dwelling** losses caused by freezing. This
   exclusion applies when the home is occupied, vacant, unoccupied or being constructed, this
   exclusion does not apply when the insured has used reasonable care to:

   1. *(1) maintain heat in the* ***building structure*** *at 55 degrees Fahrenheit or higher*

      *(2) shut off the water supply and drain the system and appliances of water, (unless you
      have an automatic fire protective sprinkler system, in which case you must use reasonable
      care to maintain the water supply and maintain heat in the building structure at 55 degrees
      or higher for coverage to apply.)*

   Consider factors such as: the steps taken by the insured to maintain the heat at 55 degrees or
   higher, the time of year, geography and circumstances of each loss when determining whether
   the insured has used reasonable care.

   If the insured has complied with policy provisions, we will pay the reasonable cost actually
   incurred to thaw frozen pipes and drainage systems if owned by or the responsibility of the
   insured.

   If necessary to excavate and refill the ground to correct the frozen plumbing, we will pay the
   necessary costs of the excavation and refill in addition to the cost to thaw and repair the
   system.

### B. Coverage B – Personal Property Frozen Pipes

1. HW 2100 Homeowners Series

   When evaluating coverage for a **Coverage B – Personal Property** claim resulting from frozen
   pipes review **Section – I Losses Insured 14**. This peril applies when the home is occupied,
   vacant, unoccupied or being constructed if the insured has used reasonable care to:

   a. maintain heat in the ***building structure*** at 55 degrees Fahrenheit or higher

   b. shut off the water supply and drain system and appliances of water (unless you have an
      automatic fire protective sprinkler system, in which case you must use reasonable care to
      maintain the water supply and maintain heat in the building structure at 55 degrees or
      higher for coverage to apply.

   Consider factors such as: the steps taken by the insured to maintain the heat at 55 degrees or
   higher, the time of year, geography and circumstances of each loss when determining whether
   the insured has used reasonable care.

   If the insured uses reasonable care to maintain the heat in the building structure at 55
   degrees or higher and still sustains a loss caused by freezing, we will pay the reasonable
   cost to repair or replace the damaged appliance.

   In addition, portable hot tubs or portable spas are covered under named peril 14 when the
   insured has used reasonable care to prevent freezing.

### C. Swimming Pools and Hot Tubs/Jacuzzis - Freeze Damage

Refer to OG 75-100 Claims Applications – First Party Section III G. Swimming Pools for guidance
related to whether or not the swimming pool would be considered **Coverage A – Dwelling or
Coverage B – Personal Property**.

1. If it is determined the swimming pool or hot tub/jacuzzi is **Coverage A – Dwelling**,
   coverage for damage due to freezing would extend to the connecting water line, however there
   would be no coverage for the pool or hot tub/jacuzzi, including the filtration and circulation
   systems as outlined in **Section I – Losses Not Insured**, 1.c.

   - HW-2100

     **Section I – Losses Not Insured** 1.c. to include snow and sleet. Under this policy we
     would not have coverage for damage caused by freezing, thawing, pressure or weight of water,
     ice, snow or sleet whether driven by wind or not. Under this policy damage caused to a
     **Coverage A – Dwelling** pool cover caused by the weight of snow, ice, or sleet would not
     be covered.

2. A portable hot tub/swimming pool or jacuzzi is **Coverage B – Personal Property** and will be
   covered for freezing under **Section I – Losses Insured Coverage B 14. Freezing** as it is
   considered a "household appliance."

   Refer to Section VII C. 1. b. (HW2100) above for guidance.

## VIII. WINTER WEATHER CLAIMS INVOLVING: ICE DAMS, WIND OR WEIGHT OF ICE, SNOW OR SLEET

### A. Ice Dams

1. **Coverage A – Dwelling**

   In areas where there is considerable snowfall and extended sub-freezing temperatures, ice dams
   will sometimes form on the lower edges of the roof above the gutters. Under certain thawing
   and freezing conditions, this can cause water to back up under the shingles and enter the
   interior of the dwelling. Water damage to the dwelling would be covered.

2. **Coverage B – Personal Property**

   Personal property would be subject to the named perils listed in the policy.

   Weight of ice, snow or sleet

   The Coverage B peril most closely related to the ice dam or freezing and thawing of snow on a
   roof can be contributed to the weight of ice, snow, or sleet which causes damage to property
   contained in a building or structure (HW 2100).

   In an ice dam situation, water may enter the dwelling and can damage personal property which
   would not be covered under peril 11, weight of ice, snow or sleet.

   However, when the investigation establishes the weight of ice, snow, or sleet caused damage to
   the building, and through this damaged area, water from the snow, ice, or sleet enters and
   damages personal property, coverage would apply under peril 11.

### B. Windstorm

Windstorm or hail. Damage to personal property in a building or structure caused by snow would be
covered only if the windstorm damaged the building or structure causing an opening in a roof or
wall and the snow enters through this opening.

If no windstorm was associated with the snow, the windstorm peril would not apply.

### C. Free-standing Carports

1. HW-2100 Homeowner Series

   A free-standing carport that is fully enclosed and permanently affixed to the real estate
   would be considered a ***building structure*** as defined and qualify as an ***Other
   Structure***. A carport which is a ***building structure*** would be covered for collapse due
   to the weight of ice and snow. If not permanently attached, they are covered as personal
   property subject to named perils. The individual facts of each free-standing carport must be
   considered, and judgment applied to determine the Coverage A or Coverage B status.

### D. Flat Roofs

1. Flat roofs may contain standing water whether or not there is build-up of ice and/or snow, due
   to the nature of construction. Some leaks through flat roofs are caused by deteriorated roofing
   (rather than action of freezing and thawing) in which case roof damage may not be covered,
   unless caused by some other covered peril.

2. If the inspection reveals ice and snow damage caused the leaks, payment for the roof should be
   considered. Some investigations may require an expert. For additional information.

   Refer to: OG 75-160 Wind/Hail Roofing Guidelines.

3. Interior damage may appear some distance away from the source of the roof leak. Careful
   inspection is required in all cases to ensure we have found the actual cause of the leak,
   damage, or deterioration, and have properly paid or denied the claim.

### E. Awnings

1. HW 2100 Series

   The HW- 2100- policy has added language to **Section I – Losses Not Insured** 1.c. to include
   snow and sleet. In addition, the policy specifically includes awnings as part of the property
   not covered for damage caused by freezing, thawing, pressure or weight of water, ice, snow or
   sleet whether driven by wind or not.

### F. Removal of Snow and Ice from Roofs and Gutters

Removal of snow and ice from roofs and gutters is not covered. If the weight of ice and snow, or
other covered peril has caused structural damage to the dwelling which requires immediate repair
to protect the property from further damage, then we would pay to remove the snow and ice from
the involved area, if necessary, to make the repairs.

### G. Exterior Siding Discoloration and Stains

No coverage. Excluded by wear and tear, deterioration, contamination language.

Exterior siding discoloration and stains are a normal event related to weather conditions. The
number of such situations may increase during severe winters.

If other damage to the property has occurred and was caused by a covered peril (such as weight of
ice, snow, or sleet or collapse of the building or a part thereof), and such damage produced a
diversion of the normal flow of water (for example, melting ice), we will honor such damage along
with the covered peril damage.

### H. Additional Living Expense (ALE)

Refer to: OG 75-102 Loss of Use, Additional Living Expense, Fair Rental Value, and Prohibited Use:
Homeowners Policy.

### I. Losses by Power Interruption

Refer to: OG 75-101 Claims Interpretations – Additional Coverages Section V. Power Interruption

## IX. COMMERCIAL MULTI-PERIL (CMP 4000 SERIES) POLICIES

Commercial property claims for water losses require diligent investigation to determine the cause
of loss and application of all available coverages. Consult claim management for guidance and
review of questions arising during a claim investigation or coverage evaluation. Consider use of
a non-waiver or reservation of rights where causation may not trigger coverage.

1. **Section I – Property Subject to Limitations**, 1. e. states in part: "We will not pay for
   loss to:

   e. The interior of any building or structure, or the property inside any building or
      structure, caused by rain, snow, sleet, ice, sand or dust, whether driven by wind or not,
      unless:

      1. The building or structure first sustains damage by a Covered Cause of Loss to its roof,
         outside walls, or outside building glass through which the rain, snow, sleet, ice, sand
         or dust enters; or

      2. The loss is caused by thawing of snow, sleet or ice on the building or structure."

   Water entering and causing damage to the interior of the building through the action of
   freezing and thawing is not "rain, snow, sleet, sand or dust." These losses will be covered
   for the action of freezing and thawing of snow causing an ice dam and resultant interior
   damage.

2. Swimming Pools and Hot Tubs/Jacuzzis- Freeze Damage

   CMP 4100 policies also exclude freeze losses to swimming pools under **Section I – Property
   Subject to Limitations**, 1. f.

3. FLOOD, SURFACE WATER, AND SUBSURFACE WATER EXCLUSION

4. BACK-UP FROM SEWER, DRAIN OR SUMP

   Under the CMP 4000 Series Policies there is no coverage for damage caused by water or sewage
   that backs up or overflows from a sewer, drain or sump except as discussed below.

   A. Drain Blockage and Fill-Up

      Coverage questions have arisen when a drain or sewer line on a described premise becomes
      plugged or blocked for various reasons. Water then fills up behind this blocked drain from
      within the interior of the structure. As a result of this blockage, the drain line fills up
      and overflows.

      Example: A drain trap underneath a lavatory becomes plugged and the faucet is accidentally
      left on, which causes the bowl to overflow. This is a "fill-up" situation and coverage
      should be extended for this accidental discharge, leakage, or overflow of water.

      For coverage to apply, domestic water from an internal source must fill up through the
      sewer or drain due to blockage of the system.

   B. Back-up of Sewer or Drain Endorsement

      Back-up of sewer or drain endorsements are available in some states. Applicable
      endorsements which are part of the policy are to be reviewed when a claim is presented to
      verify coverage, special limits and deductibles.

      A typical back-up of sewer or drain endorsement provides coverage for accidental direct
      physical damage to covered property described under **Coverage B – Business Personal
      Property** when damaged directly and immediately and solely by water or sewage that enters
      through a sewer or drain located inside the interior of the structure, or enters through
      and overflows from within a sump pump, sump pump well, or any other system located inside
      the interior of the structure, and designed to remove subsurface water drained from the
      foundation area.
