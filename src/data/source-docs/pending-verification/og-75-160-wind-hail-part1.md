## I. PURPOSE

This Operation Guide provides guidelines for handling roof claims consistent with Our Commitment to Our Policyholders and the Preface. In general, the guidance provided is applicable to all policy forms unless otherwise indicated in the Operation Guide. Review the policy form and/or endorsement(s) to confirm the specific contract language and/or limits which are applicable to an individual claim.

If a policy provision or application is more restrictive than the law of the jurisdiction, then the law of the jurisdiction will apply. Also, there may be situations where the Enterprise position is broader than the law of the jurisdiction. In such situations, you should follow the Enterprise position. If policy wording in the jurisdiction conflicts with any of these guidelines or in case of unusual claim situations, contact claim management.

## II. INVESTIGATION/INSPECTION GUIDELINES

### A. When to Inspect

Wind: Wind claims involving minor damage may be handled by phone. A physical inspection may be necessary when damage is reported to multiple slopes, when interior damage is reported, or when the age and condition of the roof would make the roof more susceptible to damage.

Hail: Other than noted below, a physical inspection should be completed on all claims reported with potential hail damage.

A physical on-roof inspection should be completed when safe and practical to do so.

Hail claims involving large hail may be eligible for the Potential Large Hail (PLH) inspection workflow. The PLH inspection workflow identifies claims where a roof inspection may not be required based on the weather data assessment on the date of loss at the loss location.

The PLH inspection workflow is applicable:

- If the weather data reflects hail is equal to or larger than 2.25" for a PLH inspection workflow, unless directed differently by Consulting Services, and
- An inspection of the exterior elevations of the property determines covered collateral damage consistent with hail large enough to require a replacement of the roof, a roof inspection may not be necessary.

The PLH inspection workflow is approved for use on Claim Groups, following CA&P departure from a WCCS Deployed event or losses without an established Claim Group.

Losses eligible for PLH inspection workflow:

- Homeowner, Manufactured Home and Rental Dwelling policies
- Composition roofs on the primary structure and other structures, other than impact resistant.
- When an event is determined to be eligible for PLH inspection workflow, claim handlers will inspect (virtual or physical) exterior elevations of the property for collateral damage.
- If collateral damage to the exterior elevations is found consistent with hail large enough to require a replacement of the roof, an inspection of the roof may not be required.
- The composition roof, drip edge, valley flashing, roof vents and pipe jacks should be included in the estimate.
  - Metals that transition from the roof to a wall/chimney should not be included in the estimate unless damage is noted for example step flashing, chimney flashing.
- The number of roof layers should be verified in the inspection.
- Composition roofs, other than impact resistant, on other structures are eligible for PLH inspection workflow.

Losses not eligible for PLH inspection workflow:

- Roofing types other than composition noted above.
- If no collateral damage is found to the exterior elevations, or if the collateral damage found is not of sufficient size to warrant a total roof replacement without a physical inspection of the roof, a physical inspection of the roof should be completed to evaluate for potential damage.

Verisk Weather system-generated file notes may be used to obtain data on hail or wind gust which may have occurred on the date of loss. The information in the system-generated Verisk Weather file notes may be considered as a part of the investigation of the loss.

The system-generated hail report assists claim handlers in determining the size of hail that may have occurred at the loss location. The Verisk Weather hail file note includes a 3 year summary at the loss location.

When hail at the loss location on the date of loss is 2.25" or greater, the PLH inspection workflow may be considered.

The system-generated wind gust report assists claim handlers in determining the wind gust that may have occurred at the loss location. The Verisk Weather wind gust file note includes a 1 year summary at the loss location.

Removal of snow and ice from roofs to allow for an inspection: Refer to [OG 75-20](#), Water Damage Losses Section VIII. Winter Weather Claims Involving Ice Dams or Weight of Ice, Snow or Sleet Ice F. Removal of Snow and Ice from Roofs and Gutters or guidance.

If it is necessary to meet with a contractor then arrange to meet at the insured risk. Travel to the location in the company-provided transportation.

[OG 75-07, Structural Loss Claim Handling Section II.D.1. Inspection](#) provides details on the Roof and Exterior Inspection Assist Program which is a tool to assist claim handlers with roof and exterior structural damage inspection reports.

Refer to: [OG 75-01 First Party Claims Guidelines & Requirements](#), and [OG 75-07 Structural Loss Claim Handling](#) for more information on inspections.

### B. Prior to the Inspection

Prior to the inspection, obtain information from the policyholder related to the type of damage he or she may have noted to the exterior, interior, or other building structures or appurtenances. If roof damage is reported, inquire as to the type of roof surface, age and if the home is a one or two-story structure. When a two-story or greater ladder is needed to safely complete the inspection, refer to [OG 75-07 Structural Loss Claim Handling](#). When interior damage is reported, clarify the extent of damage, for instance if the damage is to one or multiple rooms.

Weather information: When inspecting wind/hail claims, having information about the weather event can be valuable. Information which may be available includes, but is not limited to, the direction of the storm, size of hail, wind speed and amount of rainfall. This information can be obtained in various ways including weather service websites and conversations with the policyholder. Review the policy information and loss history as outlined in [OG 75-01, First Party Claim Handling](#) requirements.

Refer to: [Quality First Contact Guide - Fire](#)

### C. What to Inspect

Exterior: Inspect exterior property and note findings during the inspection. Walk completely around the risk, noting damage to any and all portions of the building, as well as any outbuildings, fences, gutters, decks, air conditioning condensing units and other structures or appurtenances. Evaluate damage or lack of damage to covered property during the inspection. Document the claim file.

Interior: When interior damage is noted, inspect the interior to evaluate if the damage is related to any damage to the roof surface. Ask the policyholder when the damage was first noticed and if there is damage to one or multiple rooms. Inspect all areas of damage. Evaluate damage or lack of damage to covered property during the inspection. Document the claim file.

Roof: Initially, inspect the overall roof to establish its general condition and extent of damage. Consider the type of storm, direction of the wind/hail, and the size/shape of the hailstones in that area. Inspecting other items such as gutters, vents, skylights, decks, outbuildings, air conditioning condensing units, and fences may assist in establishing if the roof may have sustained damage.

If the roofing is deteriorated, but no damage due to a covered peril is noted, determine if the roof can be walked on without causing substantial damage. In rare situations, it is acceptable to examine and photograph the roof from several points around the perimeter without actually walking on the roof. Questions should be reviewed with management.

During the inspection, when safe and practical to do so, document the approximate age, condition of roof materials, evidence of roofing underlayment such as felt or self-adhered waterproof membrane (commonly referred to as ice and water barrier), the number of layers, pitch of the slopes as well as the type of roof surface.

Resources available to assist in identifying the type of roof surface may include:

- The Roofing Locator Service is a tool that can assist with shingle identification for all types of roof surfaces. Refer to Section VII. Roofing Locator Service
- The policyholder may be able to provide information that may assist with the identification of the type of roof surface. For instance building records, repair receipts.

### D. Inspection Safety

At any phase of a claim investigation, circumstances may arise that make the inspection/investigation unsafe or unreasonable. This can include times when an individual is creating a distraction which creates an unsafe situation or makes completion of a thorough and complete inspection not possible. When such situations arise:

- In accordance with Workplace Security Policy take safety measures to prevent a threat or violent act from being carried out.
- If safe to do so, advise the insured:
  - You will be unable to complete the inspection/investigation, and
  - Will return or continue when the circumstances giving rise to the situation have been remedied, and
    - The inspection may be done safely without interruption

Advise your management immediately when these situations arise and report any workplace violence threats by:

- Filling out the Threat Report located in SFNet, State Farm Forms, under Administrative Services, in the Security folder, or
- Call the Threat response Hotline, available 24 hours a day at (309) 766-0911.

### E. Photographs

Photographs should give a clear indication of the extent of damage involved or lack of damage. The photographs should reflect the basis of the adjustment and support the decisions made. Judgment should be exercised to determine which photographs are sufficient for documenting the claim file.

The following are the type of roofing photos that may be secured and documented in the claim file when safe and practical to do so:

- Risk photo
- Photos depicting the number of layers on the roof surface.
- Overview photos of the entire slopes which depict the number and type of vents on the roof surface.
- Overview and close up photos of every test square when applicable. Take photos of test squares from below the test square and with the test square centered in the photo.
- Close-up photos clearly depicting damage or lack of damage.
- Close-up photos to document and depict the type of roof materials and condition of the roof. Take photos which should clearly reflect the reparability or non-reparability of the roof.
- Close-up photos clearly depicting damage or lack of damage to roof accessories (for example - vents, flashing, gutters, skylights, valleys, solar panels).
- Photos to document interior damage.
- Photos of undamaged areas pertinent to the claim presented.

Refer to: [OG 75-01, First Party Claims Guidelines & Requirements, Section III. D. 8. Photographs](#).

### F. Test Squares

Test squares should be used when inspecting all types of roof surfaces to evaluate damage resulting from hail when safe and practical to do so. Test squares are not necessary for claims involving wind damage only, since wind damage is generally apparent and not always evenly distributed over the roof.

On hail claims, test squares are usually made on a 10x10 area. Mark with a chalk/lumber crayon a 100 square foot sample area (or test square) that is representative of the damage or lack of damage found on that slope. Mark and count the number of damaged shingles within the test square. Damage is determined by the number of damaged shingles, not individual tabs. On flat roof surfaces mark and count a representative sample of the damage found within the sample area.

Hail damage on a roof can vary from slope to slope, depending on the pitch and condition of each slope and the direction of the hail. Complete test squares on a number of slopes to evaluate the damage to the entire roof. In cases where the slopes have similar damage, complete a test square for each direction. In cases where the damage varies or directional test squares are not indicative of the overall roof, complete additional test squares where needed.

Test squares are not needed in situations where the inspection indicates no hail damage exists or a total loss from heavy hail damage applies. In these situations, the damage, or lack thereof, should be apparent from the initial inspection, and documented by photographs and claim file notes.

### G. Measurements

When a physical inspection is completed and it is safe and practical to do so, measure as accurately as possible and document the measurements in the claim file as follows:

- EagleView should be used on individual claims to provide a diagram and measurements of the roof system. The EagleView report should be ordered prior to the inspection to aid in verifying the measurements of the roof system. Refer to Section II. Investigation/Inspection Guidelines H Diagrams/Sketch and I EagleView.
- Where EagleView is unable to produce a ClaimsReady Roof Report, an Adjuster Assisted 2 D (AA2D) Report, or an Adjuster Assisted 3 D (AA3D) Report, the HOVER application can be used by either the insured or the claim handler. Refer to Section II J. HOVER
- When both EagleView and HOVER are unable to produce a report, a diagram using the Sketch function within Xactimate can be used to document measurements. When Sketch is used, it is not necessary to create both a handwritten diagram and sketch.
- If any covered damage to the roof surface is noted, the entire roof should be measured and documented in the claim file.

Refer to: [OG 75-07 Structural Loss Claim Handling Section II. Structural Loss Claim Handling Procedures D. Investigation of Structural Losses 3. Diagrams/Measurements](#)

### H. Diagrams/Sketch

When an inspection is completed and covered damage is noted, a diagram of the entire roof surface should be obtained from EagleView. When EagleView is unable to produce a roof report, a roof diagram should be completed by the claim handler using the Sketch function within Xactimate if it is safe and practical to do so.

The claim file or diagram should show the following:

- Accurate measurements of the entire roof and the computations used to establish the square footage of the damaged areas. If the Xactimate program is used to calculate the damaged area or roof surface, it is not necessary to reflect the computations separately in the claim file.
- The location of damage and test squares should be indicated on the diagram, Roofing Scope Sheet, Sketch, or noted in the file through the scope notes or file notes. Other appurtenances may be indicated on the diagram or Sketch or noted in the file through the scope notes or file notes.

### I. EagleView

EagleView should be used on individual claims to provide a diagram and measurements of the roof system. EagleView is not used to determine the scope of damage and does not replace the physical inspection of the property.

When EagleView is used, the claim handler should receive a copy of the EagleView Roof Report prior to the inspection. During the inspection the claim handler should verify that the EagleView Roof Report provided is for the correct property.

1. If an EagleView ClaimsReady Roof Report is received, the claim handler should review and compare this roof report to the visual observation of the roof.
   - If these visual observations create questions with respect to accuracy of measurements contained in this roof report, the claim handler must measure the pitch and eave length of the predominate slope and compare these measurements to this roof report.
   - If the claim handler measurements do not agree with the measurements provided in this roof report, the claim handler should proceed with manually measuring the entire roof.
   - The Claims Help Desk may be notified (877-236-7097 or Home Clms-AMSHelp) in situations where the measurements on the EagleView Roof Report were corrected by a claim handler.

   If no questions/concerns arise from the comparison of the roof report to the visual observation of the roof, the claim handler should handle the loss relying upon the roof report received and document the file indicating the EagleView measurements were used.

2. If an EagleView Adjuster Assisted 3D (AA3D) Report is received, verification of the "shaded" area of the report is required.
3. If an EagleView Pre-Pitch 2D Report (EagleView Adjuster Assisted 2D (AA2D) Report) is received, verification of the pitch of each slope and eave length of one predominate slope is required. Once the verification is complete, the portal provided by EagleView can be used to generate an update report. For instructions on portal use, see EagleView Portal Access & Location Verification Job Aid.

Refer to OG 782-200 Aerial Measurement Services - EagleView. See also the [Xactimate Roof Waste Tool Job Aid](#) for additional details.

EagleView Exterior Walls Reports should be requested to assist when damage has been reported to an exterior elevation(s). Follow the instructions in the [EagleView Walls Report Usage Job Aid](#) and the [EagleView Walls Report Ordering, Usage & Xactimate Sketch Job Aid](#).

### J. HOVER

Where EagleView is unable to produce a ClaimsReady Roof Report and Adjuster Assisted 2 D (AA2D) Report, or an Adjuster Assisted 3 D (AA3D) Report, HOVER may be used on individual claims to provide a diagram and measurements of the roof system and, when needed, of each exterior elevation.

HOVER is not used to determine the scope of damage and does not replace the physical inspection of the property. HOVER may not be used for obtaining interior diagrams or measurements. The HOVER "Complete" deliverable type should be requested to assist when damage has been reported to an exterior elevation(s).

HOVER may be offered to the insured to download and submit exterior photos of the structure.

When the insured uses the HOVER application, they should be instructed to only take photos from the ground level. HOVER will generate a PDF report which includes only roof measurements or roof, exterior wall, and exterior opening measurements based on the report deliverable type specified (either Roof only or Complete). The HOVER report may be used to create a diagram in Xactimate Sketch.

If the insured declines to use HOVER application, or is unable to submit the exterior photos, the claim handler can complete the HOVER submission with a company owned device.

1. If HOVER is used for obtaining roof and exterior diagrams and measurements, the HOVER report is compared to the visual observation of the roof and/or elevations.
   - If visual observations create questions regarding measurement accuracy of the roof, the pitch and eave length of the predominate slope should be measured and compare these measurements to the HOVER report.
   - If there are questions on the accuracy of an exterior elevation, the predominate wall length should be measured and compared to the HOVER report.
2. If the manual measurements do not agree with the measurements provided in the HOVER report, proceed with manual measurements. The Claims Help Desk may be notified (877-236-7097) in situations where the measurements on the HOVER report were corrected.
3. After review, if the HOVER report provides an accurate diagram and measurements, the HOVER report may be used in the estimate.

   See the **HOVER Job Aid** for additional details.

### K. Other Appurtenances

Roof vents, skylights, gutters, downspouts, drip edge, metal valleys, flashing, patio covers, satellites, a/c condensing units and solar panels may also have sustained wind or hail damage. The gauge or thickness of the metal/aluminum or plastic materials varies from product to product making some more or less susceptible to damage. Note any damage observed to these appurtenances during the inspection as this may provide information about the size and type of damage and will help the claim handler properly account for these items when preparing the estimate.

Other appurtenances are but one piece of the investigation process in assessing whether or not wind or hail damage exists to the roof system. Keep in mind these items can be damaged by many things, including fallen trees, property debris, installation, or in some cases, footfall.

Solar panels: Solar panels located on the roof should be inspected as they may have sustained wind or hail damage. While completing the inspection document the location, type of materials used (if known), and extent of damage observed. The solar panels may need to be removed and reset to allow for repairs/replacement of the roof system. Additional investigation may be needed to evaluate the type of materials, extent of damage and repairs needed to the solar panel.

Caution should be used when inspecting solar panels as these panels may be energized.

Patio Covers: During the investigation of patio covers, examine the patio cover for a covered loss. On those claims where a covered loss exists the following information may be noted, the number of posts, special trim pieces, how the cover is attached to the structure, any special cover and if known, who installed the cover.

### L. Management Review

Management should review documentation supporting recommendation of full roof replacement when:

- Wind claims where the Verisk Weather wind gust history at the loss location identifies wind gust speeds less than or equal to 62 mph.
- Hail claims where the Verisk Weather hail size at the loss location address identifies a hail size less than or equal to 1 inch.

Management review of the claim handler recommendation should be reflected in the claim file.

## III. ADJUSTMENT GUIDELINES

### A. Repair vs. Replace

When determining whether a roof or slope should be repaired or replaced, compare the cost to repair actual damage caused by a covered peril to the cost to remove and replace the area damaged by a covered peril. For composition roofs, the cost to repair is compared to the cost to remove and replace a one-story, walk-on pitch roof. For other shingle/shake roofs, the cost to repair is compared to the cost to remove and replace the shingles/shakes of a one-story, walk-on pitch roof.

When the cost to repair actual damage to a composition roof or slope approaches or exceeds 50% of the cost to remove and replace the damaged area, determine whether the slope should be replaced or repaired. The same applies to other shingle/shake roof surfaces which are not composition, when the cost to repair the roof approaches or exceeds 80% of the cost to remove and replace the slope.

For hail losses, the following may be used to determine repair vs. replace for shingled/shake roofs of any type. This would not apply for other types of roof surfaces. If the damage found in the test square approaches or exceeds the total number of damaged shingles per square identified by the repair vs. replace calculations, consider all roofing guidelines to determine if replacement of the roof can be estimated. Damage is determined by the number of damaged shingles, not individual tabs. This analysis should be completed on a slope by slope basis.

The repair vs. replace calculation is systematically created within Xactimate. The results of the calculation are based on the price list selected for each specific estimate. The chart may be accessed by following these steps in each estimate:

  i. Click 'Estimate'
  ii. Click 'Sketch'
  iii. Click 'Roof Slope Total vs Repair' in the Search window under the 'Search Price List' heading

Once the applicable roofing type has been located in the chart, the number in the Value column reflects the number of damaged shingles per square to consider when deciding whether to repair or replace a damaged slope, or the entire roof.

The repair vs. replace analysis outlined above should be used when adjusting hail losses where the damage is found randomly across the slope(s).

For wind losses, test squares do not apply. Wind damage may be concentrated to a specific area(s) of the slope(s) or may be widespread and scattered damage throughout a slope(s). The inspection should document the total number of wind damaged shingles to each slope and the areas of wind damage.
